## What architects talk about over coffee

I still remember the conversations with other architects. We often fantasized about the features we wished we had in our software. Automation of repetitive tasks. Better teamwork integration. Auto-offsets for calculating housing usable floor area.

We knew exactly what we needed. But had absolutely no say in whether it would ever be built.

Of all those conversations, the one that sticks with me the most was with a representative from one of the software companies. We were complaining about a new icon set. Aesthetic, but way less readable. He answered honestly: the company had just spent a fortune on over 800 new icons, so the topic was practically closed for the next years.

I still remember the thought that popped into my head:

_"Beautiful. We're going to have to live with this for years."_

I could know exactly how something should work, but couldn't do a single thing about it.

Helplessness - doesn't feel good.

## First night with JS

It started small. Exhausted after work, I fired up a free JavaScript course. Variables, loops, conditions, a bit of basic math. Two hours later, I had it — wrote a script that automated calculations that would normally take me half the evening.

Now it makes me smile: I was so green back then. Had no idea how to even run the code. Just read somewhere that browsers have their own terminals. So, for the first few days, was running it inside Chrome and Firefox dev tools.

Still, the feeling was phenomenal. If I could write something like this after just a few hours, what would I be able to create after years of growth in this direction?

## An idea that wouldn't let me go

It came to me out of nowhere while working on a pure HTML + CSS project. The main pain point wasn't flexbox or responsiveness — it was choosing the color palette. Looking at the color pickers available online, I thought: how about building something like this, but integrating it with the project files, so every change on the color wheel is instantly applied live to the dev files?

At the HTML+CSS stage, there was no way I could write this, but returned to the idea while learning React. The satisfaction of building a great-looking, working UI/UX was massive. But one problem came up:

_"I only know frontend."_

And I was reading that to actually build this project, I needed things I knew absolutely nothing about. Node fs, databases, API calls, etc. Otherwise, couldn't build the tool from my fantasies.

_"I need backend."_

It might sound obvious, but back then, it was a turning point. Before that, I thought about going purely into front-end, but realization was clear, back-end is also needed. Actually, I needed both, otherwise, wouldn't feel fulfilled. Which meant a new direction -> fullstack.

It took me a few years to fully understand how important that moment was — but this is s topic for another time.

## Fast forward: My first dev tool

The color palette project went on a hiatus for a while. But it came back, much later. By then, I already had my first larger full-stack application in Next.js running on my VPS — CRUD, DB queries, API calls, validation, user accounts, all that jazz.
Felt that this was the moment to go back to that tool and bring it to life, in its full version.

Going back to it was funny in itself. Looking at the code, could see what I didn't know back then, but also that I had really put effort into the parts I did know. There was some refactoring to do, but also a lot of pride in my "past self." I integrated what was good and built a fully functional full-stack app.

My very own first dev tool.

What I had dreamed of as an architect was now within my reach.

I could build tools for myself and other specialists in my industry.

The sense of agency and power was intoxicating.

**And the best part — it was just the beginning.**
